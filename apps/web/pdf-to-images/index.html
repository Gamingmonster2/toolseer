PDF to Images

Render every page — or just the ones you choose — to PNG or JPEG at 72, 150 or 300 DPI. Download the images one by one or all together as a ZIP.

How pages become images

Each selected page is drawn onto a canvas at the resolution you picked, then encoded as PNG or JPEG. The DPI setting is a scaling factor, not a magic sharpener: the page is rendered at dpi divided by 72 times its natural size, so a 300-DPI export of an A4 page comes out at about 2480 × 3507 pixels. Choosing a higher DPI than the content actually contains does not add detail — a low-resolution scan exported at 300 DPI is simply a larger copy of the same low-resolution picture.

The two formats trade size against fidelity. PNG stores every rendered pixel losslessly, which suits text, diagrams and screenshots and produces the largest files. JPEG compresses hard and is the right choice for photo-heavy pages; the Image quality slider, from 0.5 to 1.0, sets how much detail JPEG discards and applies to JPEG output only. Pages are rendered onto a white background, matching how a PDF reader shows them.

The Pages field is optional. Leave it empty and every page is exported. Type a range such as 1-3, 5, 8-12 and only those pages are rendered, in the order you wrote them; a page listed twice appears once. Output files are named toolseer-page-01.png, toolseer-page-02.png and so on (with .jpg for JPEG), and when there is more than one image the result panel offers a Download all (.zip) button alongside individual downloads. The ZIP is assembled in your browser from the images it just made — nothing is sent anywhere.

What image export is not

An image is a picture of a page, not the page itself: text becomes pixels and is no longer selectable or searchable, and the original PDF is never modified. If you need the text, use the Extract Text tool instead. And because every rendered image is held in memory at once, a very long document at 300 DPI can exhaust a browser tab — for jobs like that, the ToolSeer desktop app renders page by page to disk.

Frequently asked questions

Are my files uploaded to a server?

No. Conversion runs entirely in your browser. The PDF is read from disk, each page is drawn onto a canvas, and the images are handed back as downloads. Nothing is transmitted anywhere.

What does the DPI setting really do?

It sets the rendering resolution. A page is drawn at dpi divided by 72 times its natural size, so a 300-DPI export of an A4 page is about 2480 by 3507 pixels. Higher DPI means larger, sharper-looking images — but if the page contains a low-resolution scan, upscaling cannot invent detail that is not there.

PNG or JPEG — which should I choose?

PNG is lossless: every rendered pixel is stored exactly, which suits text, diagrams and screenshots but makes larger files. JPEG is much smaller for photo-heavy pages and trades some sharpness for size. The quality slider, from 0.5 to 1.0, applies to JPEG only.

Can I export only some pages?

Yes. Type a range such as 1-3, 5, 8-12 in the Pages field and only those pages are rendered, in that order. Leave the field empty to export every page.

Will the images contain selectable text?

No. An image export is a picture of each page: text becomes pixels, so it cannot be selected or searched. If you need the text itself, use the Extract Text tool instead.
