Convert Images to PDF

Drag and drop your JPG or PNG files. Rearrange them in the perfect order, adjust page size, margins and compression, then download your PDF instantly — without uploading anything.

How image to PDF conversion works here

Each image you add becomes exactly one page. JPEG and PNG files are embedded straight into the PDF container without being re-encoded, which means the pixels inside the PDF are byte-for-byte the pixels you started with. Other formats are drawn to a canvas first — that is the only step where your quality setting has any effect.

Page size decides the paper, not the image. With Fit to image every page is sized to its picture, so nothing is padded. With A4 or Letter the image is scaled to sit inside the margins you chose and centred on the page. Automatic orientation picks portrait or landscape per image so tall photos do not end up tiny on a landscape sheet.

The file list is the page order. Drag a thumbnail to move it; the badge in the corner always shows the page number that image will become. If you would rather not drag, focus a handle and press Alt + Arrow to move that item one step.

What to expect from the output

  • File size is roughly the sum of your images, plus a few kilobytes of PDF structure.
  • Text is not searchable. An image PDF is a picture of a document; use OCR if you need selectable text.
  • Colour and rotation are preserved as stored in each file. EXIF rotation is applied the way your browser displays it.
  • Names are not used for page order — the visible list order is what counts.

When a browser is the wrong tool

A browser tab must hold every decoded image in memory at the same time. A few hundred ordinary photos are comfortable; a thousand 12-megapixel photos are not, and the tab will slow down or be killed by the browser. The ToolSeer desktop app runs the same conversion with the same settings but writes page by page to disk, so job size stops mattering.

Frequently asked questions

Are my images uploaded to a server?

No. The conversion runs entirely in your browser. Your images are read from disk, assembled into a PDF in memory, and handed back as a download. Nothing is transmitted anywhere, and the page works with your network disconnected once loaded.

Which image formats can I convert?

JPEG and PNG are embedded directly and keep their original pixels. WebP, GIF, BMP and AVIF are converted to PNG first by your browser. Each image becomes one page, in the order shown in the file list.

Does the PDF keep the original image quality?

JPEG and PNG are embedded without re-encoding, so quality is untouched. The quality slider applies only to images your browser had to convert, and to the optional re-compression used to shrink very large photos.

How many images can I convert at once?

Hundreds of ordinary photos are fine on a desktop browser. Because every image is held in memory, a thousand high-resolution photos can exhaust a tab. For jobs that large, use the desktop app, which streams to disk.

Can I change the page order before converting?

Yes. Drag any thumbnail to a new position, or focus its handle and press Alt with the arrow keys. The number in the corner of each thumbnail is the page it will become.
