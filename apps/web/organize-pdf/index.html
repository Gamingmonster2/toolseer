Organize PDF Pages

Reorder pages by dragging the grid, rotate any page in 90° steps, and delete the ones you do not need. Save the result as a fresh PDF without uploading anything.

How the page grid works

The grid is the whole interface: every page of your PDF appears as a card, in the order the output will have. Drag a card to a new position, or focus it and press Alt + Arrow to move it one step. Each card carries a rotate-left button, a rotate-right button and a delete button. Deleted pages stay in the grid, dimmed, so you can restore them at any point before saving — the counter above the grid always shows how many pages are kept out of the total.

The two numbers on a card mean different things, on purpose. The small badge in the corner is the page's new position among the kept pages, and it updates as you drag pages around. The label on the bar is the original page number in the source file, and it never changes no matter where you move the card. A deleted page shows a dash instead of a position. Keeping the two numbers separate means the operation is always described in terms of the source document — what you see on the bar is exactly what the engine receives.

When you save, the kept pages are copied into a brand-new PDF in their grid order, with their rotations applied. Rotation is added on top of any rotation a page already carries, in 90° steps, and page content is copied without re-encoding, so nothing loses quality. Your original file is never modified. If you delete every page, there is nothing to save: the tool reports an error instead of producing an empty document.

What organizing cannot do is preserve interactive page furniture. Form fields, hyperlinks and annotations live outside the page content that is copied, so they are not carried into the organized file — treat the result as a clean, print-ready document.

When a browser is the wrong tool

The grid renders a thumbnail of every page in memory before you start, so a document with a thousand scanned pages needs a fair amount of RAM just to load. A desktop browser handles a few hundred pages comfortably. Beyond that, the ToolSeer desktop app organizes pages on disk instead of in a tab.

Frequently asked questions

Are my files uploaded to a server?

No. Organizing runs entirely in your browser. The PDF is read from disk, the reordered pages are copied into a new file in memory, and nothing is transmitted anywhere.

What do the two numbers on a page card mean?

The small badge in the corner is the page's new position in the output — it updates as you drag pages around. The label on the bar shows the original page number from the source file, which never changes. Deleted pages show a dash instead of a position.

Does organizing change my pages' content?

No. Page content is copied into the new file exactly as stored, without re-encoding, so quality is untouched. Rotation is applied to the page as a whole. Form fields, links and annotations are not carried over, and your original file is never modified.

What happens when I delete a page?

The page stays visible in the grid, dimmed, so you can restore it before saving. It is simply left out of the output. If every page is deleted there is nothing to save, and the tool tells you instead of producing an empty file.

Can I rotate all pages at once?

Yes. The Rotate all pages button above the grid turns every kept page 90 degrees counter-clockwise each time you press it. The per-page buttons turn one page at a time, and rotation is added to any rotation the page already has.
