Why the tools live in your browser

Classic PDF websites upload your document to a server, convert it there, and make you download it back. That is why they need accounts, file-size limits and privacy policies full of exceptions. ToolSeer does the opposite: the conversion engine is downloaded once with the page and runs on your own machine. Your files never travel anywhere, the tools work offline, and there is no server that could be hacked or subpoenaed — because there is no server involved.

The trade-off is memory. A browser tab must keep a document in memory while it works, which is why extremely large files are better handled by the desktop app. For everyday documents — the overwhelming majority — the browser is faster than any upload would be.

Frequently asked questions

Are my files uploaded to a server?

No. Every ToolSeer tool runs entirely in your browser. Files are read locally, processed locally, and handed back as a download. Nothing is ever transmitted to a server, and the pages keep working offline once loaded.

Is ToolSeer really free?

Yes. Because processing happens on your device, there are no server costs to pass on. The tools are free with no page limits, no watermarks and no account requirement.

What can I do with very large PDFs?

A browser tab must hold a document in memory, so files with hundreds of pages or a thousand images can exceed what a tab handles. The ToolSeer desktop app does the same work on disk with no memory ceiling.

Which browsers are supported?

Any recent Chrome, Edge, Firefox or Safari. The heavy work uses the same WebAssembly and JavaScript engines the browser itself ships, so there is nothing to install.
